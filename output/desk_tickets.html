  • 102: exactly my plan for who did what. Boss → Facilities → Accounting, with no Inventory and no Customer Service.   • 103: my plan was out of date, and the first run missed the money. I expected the reprint vendor to be blocked by invoice 501, but my approval on 101 had already paid it, so Bulldog Print Co could ship. The agents got that right. The first run never checked this, left the price “pending the owner”, and was marked resolved. After I reopened it, the re-run had the Boss decide $50 each, refused the reprint for cash, offered only the 8 on the shelf, and kept 103 open (blocked). That’s the honest result.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why

  • 102 is the clearest case. The whole job is: read lease 1, check cash, make a $2,400 request. One agent with get_lease + check_payment could do that in about 3 model calls. The team used 8 model calls and 11,774 tokens because each hand-off means a new agent re-reading instructions, and Accounting read the lease again.
  • 103 is mixed. One agent with check_stock, vendor_shipping_status, get_pricing, and check_payment could do it, and would call get_pricing once instead of 4 times (the re-run used 26,873 tokens). But 103 also shows why roles help: the first run skipped the cash check, and the fix was giving that job clearly to Accounting and the price decision clearly to the Boss. With one agent, I’d have to put both rules in one long prompt and hope it remembers both.
  • 101 is where the team makes sense. It touches stock, a vendor bill, cash, and a customer message. Splitting it keeps the money rules with Accounting and the “don’t promise a date / don’t mention our unpaid bill” rules with Customer Service. Even here, one agent could do it, but the separate jobs make it easier to check who said what.
  • My takeaway 💛: use a team when a ticket crosses money + customer + stock and the rules are different for each. For a one-area ticket, one agent with the right tools is cheaper and has fewer repeated lookups. The bigger lesson from 103 is that my own rule (“resolved = no payment waiting”) was too loose. The harness has to check the ticket is really finished, not just trust that nothing is waiting.

4. Three new CC problems this team could solve with my tools

  1. “Can I get the Bulldog tee in M instead?” Inventory: check_stock / get_stock_all_sizes (M = 5). Customer Service: draft a reply with the get_pricing price. No cash, no approval. It’s like 101 without the vendor part.
  2. “Next month’s rent reminder.” After the run, lease 1 is next due 2026-10-02. Facilities: get_lease. Accounting: get_cash_balance + check_payment. It would find that $160 can’t cover $2,400 and say so, with no request until there’s enough cash. That tests the “no negative cash” rule for real.
  3. “Restock hoodies before the next club order.” Inventory: get_stock_all_sizes (S 4, M 8, L 14, XL 6), vendor_shipping_status (5 days). Accounting: get_pricing ($22) + check_payment. Then a purchase request that I approve, and record_payment refuses it if cash is short. Right now, 12 hoodies = $264 > $160, so it would be refused.

5. Three new CC problems this team could not solve with my tools

  1. Taking the Yale AI Club’s money (a sale or deposit). If the club says yes to $1,044, nothing can record money coming in. Every tool is read-only except record_payment, which only takes cash out. This is why 103 is stuck: $160 can’t pay for the $264 reprint, so it stays open and blocked.
    Needed: a sales/orders table, a record_sale(order_id, amount) tool that adds to cash_accounts (also human-approved), and a rule change, since this homework says “cash only goes out”.
  2. A customer return or refund for a tee they already bought. There is no orders table, so no tool can check that Tauhid (or anyone) actually bought something, when, or for how much. A refund without that check would be a guess.
    Needed: an orders table plus a get_order tool, a refund payment kind in record_payment that checks the refund isn’t more than what was paid, and inventory going back up (restock_item).
  3. Actually placing the reprint with Bulldog Print Co and tracking delivery. The team can say “earliest arrival 2026-09-05”, but it can’t send the order, get a confirmation, or know whether the shirts arrived. On purpose, there’s no email or vendor tool (drafts only).
    Needed: a purchase_orders/shipments table with create_purchase_order and get_shipment_status tools, a send_message tool that only sends a draft after a human clicks Send, and maybe a Purchasing agent that owns vendor orders, so Inventory just reports stock.

💛 The rule changes in #1 and #2 (letting cash come in, refunds) are my choice for a future version. In this homework, cash only goes out, and I kept it that way.
